# Duelist Style
**Category**: Physical  
**Primary Stat**: Dexterity or Strength (player's choice at Tier I)  
**Counter**: None hard — but unfocused builds waste the Combo system's potential  
**Countered By**: Enemies that can block combo momentum (Fortitude's *Shake Off*, high-evasion builds that deny first hit)

## Overview
The Duelist is the system's most flexible physical rank. Where Colossus does one thing perfectly and Assassin requires elaborate setup, Duelist adapts. Every fight is a different dance — reading the opponent, chaining techniques, and shifting between aggressive and defensive modes mid-round.

The core mechanic is the **Combo Counter**. Certain abilities build the counter; others spend it for enhanced effects. There is no single correct sequence — the right combo depends on what the enemy is doing. A Duelist who can't adapt their chain to the situation loses the combo's advantage, which is the rank's main weakness: it rewards skilled play and punishes predictability.

Duelists have medium damage, medium defense, and uniquely can switch between offense and defense within the same turn.

## Core Passive — Fencer's Rhythm
When you deal at least 1 Hit to an enemy on your turn, gain 1 **Combo Point**. When you take a Hit from an enemy, lose 1 Combo Point. Your Combo Points reset to 0 at the start of each of your turns. Maximum 5 Combo Points at once.

## Tier I — Initiate

**Advance** *(1 action)*  
Move up to your full speed and make a basic attack dealing 12 damage. If this attack hits and you have 2+ Combo Points, deal +5 bonus damage.

**Feint** *(1 action)*  
Make a fake attack gesture. The target must make a Perception check (DC 11) or lose their next **reaction** (they misallocate their defensive effort). Whether or not the feint succeeds, gain 1 Combo Point.

**Parry** *(reaction)*  
Trigger: you are targeted by a melee attack. Roll a Defense check with advantage. If you succeed, reduce the damage by 8 before applying threshold. Gain 1 Combo Point regardless of success.

---

## Tier II — Adept

**Riposte** *(reaction, requires 2+ Combo Points)*  
Trigger: you successfully defend against a melee attack (via Parry or a defense roll). Immediately make a counter-attack dealing 18 damage. Spend 2 Combo Points.

**Flurry** *(2 actions)*  
Make 3 quick attacks against the same target in succession, each dealing 10 damage. Each attack is rolled separately. Each hit that deals a Hit grants 1 Combo Point (up to the normal maximum). The target cannot use Parry-type reactions against more than the first attack.

**Lunge** *(1 action, requires 3+ Combo Points)*  
Dash up to 2 spaces and make a single powerful strike dealing 22 damage. Spend 3 Combo Points. If this reduces the target to 4+ Hits, they are **staggered** (lose 1 action next turn).

**Footwork** *(free action, once per turn)*  
Move up to 2 spaces without spending actions or provoking reactions. Gain 1 Combo Point if you end adjacent to an enemy.

---

## Tier III — Master

**Blade Tempest** *(3 actions, requires 4+ Combo Points)*  
An extended sequence of attacks and movement. Make 4 attacks against targets of your choice (you may split between multiple enemies), each dealing 14 damage. Spend all Combo Points on activation. For each 2 Combo Points spent, one attack in the sequence ignores the target's defense roll (automatic hit).

**Counter Stance** *(1 action)*  
Enter a defensive fighting posture until your next turn. While active: you have advantage on all defense checks, and every time you succeed a defense check against a melee attack you automatically gain 2 Combo Points and may make a free Quick Attack (8 damage) as a free action. Counter Stance ends if you move more than 1 space or use a 2+ action ability.

**Master's Rhythm** *(passive upgrade)*  
You no longer lose Combo Points when you take Hits. Additionally, your Combo Point maximum increases to 7. When you reach 7 Combo Points, you immediately gain 1 **free action** (usable once per turn) that does not reset the counter.

---

## Design Notes
- Duelist is intentionally the "learning curve" rank for players new to combo systems. Even bad combos deal reasonable damage; good combos are excellent.
- The jack-of-all-trades identity means Duelists can multi-rank effectively with almost anything. They don't contradict any other rank's mechanics.
- Flurry + Riposte is the basic bread-and-butter sequence. Blade Tempest should feel like a reward for a well-managed fight.
- *Counter Stance* is the defensive option — a Duelist who goes purely offensive is fragile. Rewarding players who mix modes is the design intent.
